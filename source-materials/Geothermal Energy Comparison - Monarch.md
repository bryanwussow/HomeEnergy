# Geothermal Energy Usage Comparison — Monarch Air, Heat & Geothermal

**Note:** Text extract of `Geothermal_Energy_Comparison_Monarch_Updated.pdf`. Prepared by Andrew Edwards, Monarch Air, Heat & Geothermal (704-237-0113), for Bryan Wussow, 6960 Old Ridge Road, Waxhaw, NC 28173.

## Purpose
Uses the Energy Saver North Carolina audit as baseline and compares expected annual energy usage for two quoted geothermal packages: a 2-ton WaterFurnace 7 Series package and a 2.5-ton WaterFurnace 5 Series package, each with a desuperheater piped into the existing 50-gallon electric water heater. Compares open-loop vs. horizontal closed-loop operation.

## Audit Baseline (source: Energy Saver NC audit, Mar 26, 2026 — weather-normalized modeled values, not necessarily exact historic usage)
- Modeled current electric usage: 10,101 kWh/year
- Modeled current wood usage: 1.09 cords/year
- Modeled current total energy cost: $1,854/year
- Audit electric rate: $0.1511/kWh
- Audit wood cost: $300/cord
- Existing heat pump size: 30,000 Btu/h (2.5 tons)
- Heating design load: 24,661 Btu/h
- Cooling load: 24,132 sensible + 1,629 latent Btu/h ≈ 25,761 total Btu/h

## Estimated Annual Energy Cost by Option
(Desuperheater = hot-water assist only, not a full water heater replacement; savings included below.)

| Scenario | Loop Type | Est. Annual Electric Use | Wood Use | Est. Annual Energy Cost | Savings vs. Baseline |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Current audit baseline | Existing air-source / wood / electric tank | 10,101 kWh | 1.09 cords | ~$1,854 | — |
| 2-ton WaterFurnace 7 Series + desuperheater | Horizontal closed loop | ~6,450–6,850 kWh | 0 cords | ~$975–$1,035 | ~$820–$880/yr |
| 2-ton WaterFurnace 7 Series + desuperheater | Open loop | ~6,250–6,700 kWh | 0 cords | ~$945–$1,010 | ~$845–$910/yr |
| 2.5-ton WaterFurnace 5 Series + desuperheater | Horizontal closed loop | ~6,650–7,050 kWh | 0 cords | ~$1,005–$1,065 | ~$790–$850/yr |
| 2.5-ton WaterFurnace 5 Series + desuperheater | Open loop | ~6,450–6,900 kWh | 0 cords | ~$975–$1,045 | ~$810–$880/yr |

**Main takeaway:** With the desuperheater tied into the existing 50-gallon electric water heater, expected annual energy cost drops from the audit's modeled $1,854/year to roughly $945–$1,065/year depending on model and loop type — an estimated savings of ~$790–$910/year, or roughly $65–$75/month averaged over the year.

## Desuperheater Impact
| Item | Estimated Impact |
|---|---|
| Existing water heater | 50-gallon electric tank, retained |
| Desuperheater role | Hot-water assist only |
| Estimated annual water-heating offset | ~500–800 kWh/year |
| Estimated dollar savings | ~$75–$120/year at $0.1511/kWh |

Desuperheater helps most during cooling season (heat rejected from home); in heating season the benefit is smaller since the heat is being produced by the geothermal system itself.

## Open Loop vs. Horizontal Closed Loop
| Comparison Point | Expected Difference |
|---|---|
| Open-loop operating advantage | Typically modest — entering water temps can be very stable |
| Estimated HVAC kWh reduction vs. horizontal loop | ~3%–8% before pumping details |
| Estimated net annual electric difference | ~150–400 kWh/year |
| Estimated annual dollar difference | ~$25–$60/year at audit electric rate |
| Main deciding factors | Water quality, well capacity, discharge method, local permitting, maintenance risk, long-term reliability |

Open loop should not be oversold as a large energy saver — the energy difference is likely smaller than the difference in installation requirements, water management, and maintenance risk. Horizontal closed loop is often the cleaner long-term ownership story unless well water quality/discharge setup are excellent.

## Sizing & Package Notes
Audit load is close to 2 tons, but existing air-source system is 2.5 tons. The 2-ton 7 Series package may be appropriate due to variable-capacity operation, but exact output should be confirmed against heating/cooling design loads at expected entering water temperature. The 2.5-ton 5 Series is the safer capacity match to the existing system size and audit cooling load, though it may not provide the same modulation/part-load comfort as the 7 Series.

## Recommended Positioning (installer's framing)
Recommend the 2-ton 7 Series if load calc, unit capacity tables, loop design, and auxiliary heat strategy confirm it covers the home at design conditions. Recommend the 2.5-ton 5 Series for the safer sizing match and likely lower installed cost. Both options should materially reduce annual energy use vs. the audit baseline, especially with wood use eliminated and desuperheater offsetting some electric water heating.

**Caveat from source doc:** "This is a budget-level comparison for discussion only. Final usage depends on equipment submittals, actual loop design, entering water temperatures, pumping watts, thermostat settings, auxiliary heat use, duct performance, domestic hot-water usage, and homeowner operating behavior."
